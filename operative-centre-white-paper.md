# The Study of Completed Intelligibility
## The Operative Centre and a Proposal for a New Research Field

**White paper · 20 October 2026 · Version 1.0**  
**Independent synthesis by Codex, developed from the TitanicParker research archive at the invitation of its creator**

### Abstract

People routinely understand utterances without being able to specify the organisation through which those utterances become sufficient as intelligible wholes. This paper proposes **completed-intelligibility studies** as a working name for a research field concerned with that organisation: its recovery, its variations, its persistence and its transmission. The central investigational concept is the **Operative Centre**, defined in the source archive as the minimal governing determination through which an unfolding intelligibility becomes sufficient and holds as achieved. Its importance lies in identifying the achievement whose structure is to be explained. The archive proposes three simultaneous dimensions of that achievement—Transformation Pattern, Completion Topology and Persistence Mode—and a resulting 54-cell grammar. This paper distinguishes the phenomenon, the centre-recovery method, the dimensional proposal and the exhaustive-taxonomy claim. It argues that the first two warrant systematic investigation without requiring advance acceptance of the latter two. It outlines a research programme combining close analysis, independent annotation, controlled variation, multilingual comparison and prospective tests. The result is a proposal for a field with a definite object and a revisable theoretical framework, rather than a declaration that its founding theory has already been established.

### 1. The phenomenon and the question

An utterance unfolds. Contributions accumulate, qualify one another and sometimes alter the significance of what came before. At some point, enough has been determined for a reader or listener to take something as achieved. The achievement may be a distinction, a dependency, a corrected understanding, a coherent whole, an available possibility or a discharged demand.

Understanding commonly proceeds without explicit awareness of that organisation. Someone can grasp an explanation while struggling to say exactly what makes it explanatory; recognise that a sentence has landed while misidentifying what it establishes; or paraphrase its topic while losing its governing achievement.

The research question is: **through what governing determination does this particular intelligibility become sufficient, and what remains achieved once it does?**

This question distinguishes several matters often compressed into the word “meaning.” We can ask what words conventionally signify, what an author intends, what a proposition asserts, whether an assertion is true, and what a listener infers. The proposed inquiry asks how the contributions constitute a sufficiently determinate accomplishment. Its relationship to those other questions must be investigated, rather than settled by vocabulary.

Completion here is local and demand-relative. It does not mean final knowledge, unquestionable truth or the impossibility of further development. A false claim can be intelligible. A conditional can be complete without its antecedent being realised. “Tomorrow” can answer a question sufficiently, while an elaborate grammatical sentence can leave its governing demand unresolved.

The object of study is therefore an intelligible accomplishment under specified conditions. Punctuation supplies a convenient initial boundary; analysis must establish the functional boundary.

### 2. Why the Operative Centre matters

The source definition is precise: the Operative Centre is “the minimal governing determination through which an unfolding intelligibility becomes sufficient and holds as achieved.” [1]

Each term carries a constraint. **Minimal** excludes dispensable material while retaining everything constitutive. **Governing** distinguishes the whole's achievement from subordinate accomplishments. **Determination** identifies what becomes sufficiently definite. **Through which** asks how the contributions make that achievement possible. **Holds as achieved** requires the account to identify the completed result, including dependencies essential to its identity.

The centre can be distributed across a sentence. It need not coincide with its subject, main verb, last word or most conspicuous image. “Centre” denotes governance, and does not imply a privileged physical or grammatical point. A distributed centre can therefore be the object of a distributed-completion analysis without contradiction.

Consider the archive's example: “The shelf holds weight because its parts brace one another.” The noun identifies the object. The connective marks an explanatory dependency. A centre account must recover the load-bearing capacity made intelligible through the organisation of mutually bracing parts. Merely naming the shelf, support or explanation leaves substantial work undone. [2]

The centre is also more than an economical summary. A good summary may preserve what a text is about while omitting the particular dependency that makes its achievement sufficient. Conversely, a centre record may require several carefully specified fields. Minimality concerns indispensable determination, not the fewest words.

This yields the paper's principal interpretive claim: **the Operative Centre identifies the common achievement to which every subsequent structural description must answer.** Without it, an analyst can take one feature from the opening, another from a subordinate clause and another from a possible consequence, then assemble a classification of no single achievement.

### 3. From an archive to a research programme

The supplied archive develops this problem through several modes of inquiry. *Persistence* articulates a constraint grammar and distinguishes generated form, canonical naming and concrete realisation. *The Manifold of Completed Intelligibility* and *See Meaning Happen* examine completion, traversal, survival and inheritance. *Access* develops source-preserving analysis and separation between an initial assay and coordinate decoding. *Pixels of Clarity*, *Very Own Repo* and *Maya's Bike* explore how attention to these distinctions can be taught through ordinary encounters. *Intelligbility* and *Looks Different This Way* investigate relational grounds, factorisation and the formation of larger intelligible worlds. [3–13]

These works should be read as a developing archive. Earlier and later terminology differs; some formulations assert stronger uniqueness or necessity than later research qualifications support. Canonical names also require version control. A coherent research programme must preserve these differences rather than treat every historical statement as one simultaneous doctrine.

*Everything Fresh* makes centre recovery explicit and gives it a procedural threshold. A source-based centre record is confirmed before classification. The subsequent classifier is restricted to that frozen record. [1, 2, 14]

My interpretation is that this makes an existing concern with governance into a more sharply specified research object. The archive had already distinguished unresolved demand, governing movement, landing and survivor. The centre brings their unity into focus: what one achieved determination connects these descriptions?

This shift changes the explanatory order. The practical starting point becomes recovery of the achievement. The proposed grammar is then tested against it.

### 4. Four claims that research must distinguish

The programme contains claims of different strength. Treating them separately protects its explanatory ambition and makes failures informative.

| Claim | What would support it | What would challenge it |
|---|---|---|
| Completed intelligibilities exhibit investigable governing organisation. | Explicit accounts explain preserved and defeated achievements under controlled changes. | Accounts add no discriminating information beyond ordinary summaries. |
| Operative Centres can be recovered with useful reliability. | Independent analysts converge on achievements and dependencies, with identifiable disagreement boundaries. | Persistent incompatible accounts survive adequate contextual specification and training. |
| Three proposed dimensions provide a useful structural factorisation. | They capture distinct variations and improve explanation or prediction over simpler descriptions. | Dimensions collapse together, omit recurrent distinctions or fail to improve competing models. |
| The 54 cells exhaustively and uniquely classify the relevant domain. | Broad prospective coverage, stable boundaries and principled treatment of difficult cases. | Reproducible out-of-system cases, unresolved overlap or systematic dependence on arbitrary framing. |

The first claim does not establish the fourth. Equally, revising the fourth need not erase evidence for the first two. A field can retain its object while changing its taxonomy.

“New field” is used here as a proposal to organise inquiry. This paper does not establish historical priority for all its ideas, institutional recognition, or the inadequacy of existing disciplines. Those are different questions requiring a wider scholarly investigation.

### 5. The proposed dimensional grammar

The archive proposes three simultaneous constraints on a single completion. Their values yield 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 formal combinations. [2, 3]

| Dimension | Question | Proposed values |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation Pattern | What kind of unresolvedness is overcome? | Differentiation, Opening, Relating, Repair, Integration, Settlement |
| Completion Topology | Through what structural condition does that overcoming become sufficient? | Focal, Distributed, Release |
| Persistence Mode | In what manner does the achieved result remain available? | Standing, Bearing, Efficacy |

These questions are accessible starting points, not sufficient instructions for practising the method. Their application requires the craft of recovering a particular achievement and discriminating it from close alternatives.

The dimensions reciprocally constrain one another. A transformation is this transformation as completed through this topology and retained in this mode. They are neither a temporal recipe nor detachable tags.

Several distinctions are especially important. Multiple nouns do not establish Distributed completion. A single physical bearer does not establish Focal completion. Release concerns a prior governor losing determining force, rather than mere absence or movement. A relation used to establish a result does not automatically mean that the result's achieved identity consists in constitutive Bearing. Practical relevance does not by itself establish Efficacy.

Likewise, the fact that every completion becomes coherent does not make every completion Integration; the fact that something is completed does not make every transformation Settlement. These values must discriminate governing achievements.

Fifty-four combinations are an arithmetic consequence of the proposed value sets. Their empirical adequacy, exclusivity and recoverability remain substantive hypotheses. The archive's mature distinction between a generated structural form, its canonical crystallisation and a particular realisation also prevents a cell name from functioning as an automatic semantic answer. [2, 3]

### 6. A working method and its limits

The current protocol separates source interpretation from taxonomic inference. Its six confirmation questions ask what was unresolved, what changed, what made that change sufficient, where sufficiency is constituted, what remains achieved and what would invalidate it. [14]

The initial record must retain scope, negation, qualification, modality and indispensable dependencies. It should identify what the full utterance achieves without importing a causal mechanism, successful performance, intention or capacity that the source does not establish.

The method then tests coverage, minimality, determinacy and non-invention. Deletion and alteration can expose omitted contributions, but their interpretation requires care. Removing a necessary term can destroy many kinds of achievement; that alone does not tell us how the original result holds. A source-preserving account must distinguish prerequisites for establishing a result from dependencies constitutive of its achieved identity.

After confirmation, the frozen centre record becomes the sole sentence-specific input to classification. This one-way threshold makes a possible error visible: a proposed cell cannot quietly rewrite the source analysis that supposedly supports it. If the record is defective, a separately preserved revision must reopen source analysis.

The A-007 seed case illustrates the value of such revision. An initial account privileged possession grammar. A later account identified the instructional contributions as constituting one articulated endowment, substantially changing the proposed coordinate. The record preserves both interpretations and explains the change. [15]

This is evidence that the procedure can expose consequential interpretive differences. It is not, by itself, independent proof that the revised account is uniquely correct. That distinction should accompany every worked example.

Understanding these requirements is also different from possessing the skill to satisfy them. The present paper proposes research and synthesises the framework; it does not certify its author's independent competence in centre recovery.

### 7. What would make this a distinct field?

A field needs a stable question, shareable objects of examination, explicit methods and a programme of cumulative disagreement. Completed-intelligibility studies could supply all four.

Its stable question concerns the sufficiency and achieved identity of intelligible wholes. Its initial objects are source-bound utterances under declared demands. Its methods combine centre reconstruction, controlled contrasts, frozen records and rival analysis. Its disagreements concern recoverable features: omitted qualifications, competing demands, constitutive dependencies, boundaries of completion and modes of holding.

The proposed distinctiveness is a specific conjunction of concerns: **governing determination, local sufficiency, achieved persistence and possible factorisation across domains**. It is not a claim that prior scholarship has ignored unity, context or interpretation.

One close comparator is Rhetorical Structure Theory. RST describes textual coherence through functional relations between spans, including nucleus–satellite and multinuclear structures. Its founding work and maintained introduction also make the observer's interpretive commitments explicit. [16] The comparison is instructive: centre research would need to show whether its demand-relative achieved determination provides additional explanatory information, rather than simply renaming textual centrality. Its proposed extension beyond textual spans to practical and mathematical accomplishments is a further research commitment, not an established advantage.

Other necessary conversations include pragmatics, phenomenology, theories of explanation, learning research and computational representations of meaning. This paper identifies those comparison areas without claiming to have completed their literature reviews. A serious founding programme should recruit expertise in them before claiming theoretical novelty or disciplinary independence.

### 8. A programme of decisive studies

The next research phase should favour tests that can change the theory over collections of agreeable examples.

**Study 1: Centre recovery before taxonomy.** Give independent analysts a prospectively sampled corpus with declared contexts. Withhold cell names, coordinates and prior solutions. Record their ordinary-language centres, indispensable dependencies and invalidating changes. Use a predeclared comparison rubric and independent adjudicators. Measure agreement about the achievement rather than identical wording. Include unresolved cases and abstentions in the denominator.

**Study 2: Information retained by the frozen record.** Compare classification from the source, from a frozen centre record and from an ordinary content summary. Use separate analysts and held-out items. The question is whether the centre preserves governing distinctions while reducing opportunities for lexical shortcuts. Better agreement alone is insufficient if the record has removed real ambiguity.

**Study 3: Predictive contrast.** Freeze an analysis, then predict which small variations preserve or defeat its achievement. Have independent readers evaluate those variations under the same context. Separate grammatical degradation, changed factual content and changed governing achievement. Success requires discriminating predictions beyond “changing an important word changes the meaning.”

**Study 4: Dimensional nonredundancy.** Construct candidate contrasts intended to vary one dimension while preserving the others. Independent analysts assess whether that preservation actually holds. Investigate difficult boundaries, especially Focal versus Distributed, Release versus the other topologies, and Standing versus Bearing. Failed contrasts remain part of the evidence.

**Study 5: Cross-language and contextual stability.** Compare utterances intended to preserve an achievement across languages. Assess lexical, pragmatic and structural equivalence separately. A common coordinate would not establish full translation equivalence. Conversely, different centre accounts might expose a translation change rather than a failure of the grammar. Contextual interventions should test when a change in governing demand legitimately changes the centre.

**Study 6: Prospective coverage and model comparison.** Freeze the framework before examining new domains and difficult cases. Compare the 54-cell account with a coordinate-free centre description, ordinary summaries and relevant discourse analyses. Track predictive accuracy, reliability, abstention, analytical effort and additional assumptions. The central question is whether the dimensions earn their explanatory cost.

Human and model-based investigations should be reported separately. Different GPT outputs are not automatically independent replications: shared training, prompts and exposure can create shared assumptions. Analysts already familiar with the grammar can work without a lookup, but this is procedural withholding rather than complete absence of theoretical knowledge.

### 9. Failure, revision and cumulative evidence

The field should distinguish at least four sources of difficulty: insufficient contextual evidence; failure to recover the governing achievement; failure to derive a unique coordinate from a sound centre; and inadequacy of the taxonomy itself.

These diagnoses must remain contestable. Calling every counterexample a bad centre would protect the taxonomy at the cost of investigability. Equally, treating every initial disagreement as a taxonomic failure would ignore the demands of interpretation. A research record needs explicit grounds for deciding between these possibilities.

A credible corpus should preserve exact sources, declared demands, protocol versions, initial records, prior exposure, alternatives, confidence, abstentions and dated revisions. Report exact-cell agreement separately from agreement on each dimension and from agreement on the centre. Do not remove difficult items because they lower the score.

The archive's proposed inheritance mechanisms deserve separate study. One completed achievement can become a premise, instruction, correction or resource for another. The important questions concern what must survive carrier changes, how losses become detectable and when a local revision alters a larger traversal. Sharing a coordinate does not establish sharing the same achievement or history.

The broader metaphysical proposals concerning awareness, self and the human intelligible world should likewise have their own arguments. Local success in sentence analysis would not automatically establish them; disagreement with them need not prevent investigation of local completion.

### 10. Potential value

If centre recovery proves reliable and useful, it could support teaching that distinguishes an argument's topic from its governing accomplishment; editing that exposes where an explanation loses sufficiency; translation review attentive to altered dependencies; and evaluation of model outputs that preserve vocabulary while changing what has been established.

These are proposed applications. Their value should be measured in task-specific comparisons. A shorter analysis, a more elegant label or an enthusiastic reader response does not establish improved understanding.

The deepest potential contribution is methodological. People could acquire a shared way to examine what they already understand, identify why a paraphrase is insufficient and locate disagreement without immediately treating it as disagreement about everything. That possibility warrants investigation even if the eventual grammar differs from the present 54 cells.

### 11. Founding position

The archive offers a substantial theoretical and pedagogical starting point. The Operative Centre supplies a particularly clear focus: the governing determination through which an intelligibility becomes sufficient and remains achieved. The dimensional grammar offers a definite, ambitious theory of its forms. The current procedural separation makes the relationship between these proposals more testable.

My recommendation is to establish **completed-intelligibility studies** as a provisional research programme, with **Operative Centre research** as its first experimental strand. The name is proposed here for discussion; it is not attributed to the archive's creator as an adopted disciplinary title.

Its founding commitment should be to make the phenomenon more inspectable, its methods more transmissible and its disagreements more exact. Its strongest result would be a theory that survives independent recovery and prospective contrast. Its productive failures would reveal where achievement, sufficiency or persistence requires a different account.

The invitation is therefore concrete: recover the achievement before naming its form; preserve the evidence before revising the explanation; and allow the form of the theory to answer to what investigation finds.
